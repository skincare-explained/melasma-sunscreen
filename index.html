Melasma Sunscreen Guide

From the reel: why the color of your sunscreen matters for melasma. Tinted picks, how to read the label, and a gentle routine.

Why color matters
Most white sunscreens

UV only
Tinted, with iron oxides
UV ✓ · light ✓
UV + light

Melasma darkens with light, not only UV, and even cloudy winter light counts. Iron oxides are the pigments that give a sunscreen its tint, and they help block visible light too.

Your plan, in three parts

Part 1 · Tinted SPF

Mineral, tinted, SPF 30 or higher
This is you if
You have melasma that stays dark, even in winter or on cloudy days.
How to use
Every morning, about two finger-lengths for face and neck. Reapply every 2 hours when you're outside.

Part 2 · Read the label

Find iron oxides in 10 seconds
Where to look
Turn the tube over. Skip the "Active ingredients" box and read the "Inactive ingredients" list, usually near the end.
What it's called
"Iron oxides", or the color codes below.
Names on the label
CI 77491
Red iron oxide
CI 77492
Yellow iron oxide
CI 77499
Black iron oxide

"Tinted" on the front is a good sign, but the ingredient list is the proof. A sunscreen can look beige without iron oxides, and a "sheer" one can still have them.

Part 3 · Gentle routine

If it stings, it goes
Why gentle
Irritation can make melasma darker. Stinging, burning or redness after a product is a sign to stop it.
How to use
Keep the routine short: cleanse, a soothing toner, a barrier cream, then tinted SPF in the morning.
